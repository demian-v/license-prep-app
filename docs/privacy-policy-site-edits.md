# Google Sites privacy policy — exact edits

For `https://sites.google.com/view/driveusa/privacy-policy`, which is the page
the app links to (`enhanced_subscription_card.dart:446`) and the one both
stores fetch during review. `privacy_policy.md` in this repo is **not** that
page; see the note at the bottom.

Read against the live page on **2026-09-17**. Four edits. The first one is the
only urgent one.

---

## Edit 1 — REMOVE the advertising-ID claim (do this one first)

**Find, in §2.2 Usage & Technical Data:**

> Device Identifiers: IP address, Device Model, OS version, and Advertising IDs (e.g., IDFA for iOS and AAID for Android).

**Replace with:**

```
Device & Technical Data: IP address, device model, operating system version,
and app version.

Trial Device Identifier (hashed): To make sure the free trial can only be
claimed once per device, we store a one-way hash of an identifier generated by
the App on your device. It is not your device's serial number and not an
advertising identifier, it cannot be reversed to identify your device, and it
is used for nothing except preventing repeated trials.
```

**Why this one matters most.** The App does not collect advertising
identifiers. Verified 2026-09-17: no ads SDK in `pubspec.yaml`, no
`NSUserTrackingUsageDescription` in `Info.plist`, no `AdSupport` or
`GoogleMobileAds` in `Podfile.lock`.

That claim puts you in a no-win position on the store forms. Declare IDFA in
App Store Connect and you are claiming to track users — which requires an App
Tracking Transparency prompt you do not show, and moves the app into a
category reviewers look at harder. Leave it undeclared and the form contradicts
your own policy. Either way it is a rejection risk, for a thing you do not do.

The replacement also does the second job: it names the hashed trial identifier
specifically, which is what makes declaring **Device ID** on the store forms
defensible rather than alarming.

## Edit 2 — ADD a Crash Diagnostics entry

**Add to §2.2, after the Analytics line:**

```
Crash Diagnostics: If the App crashes or hits an unexpected error, we collect a
diagnostic report through Firebase Crashlytics. It contains the error and the
sequence of code that produced it, your device model and operating system
version, the App version, and whether the device was jailbroken or rooted. The
report also carries your account identifier, your selected state, your
language, and whether your subscription or trial was active at the time, so
that we can reproduce the fault and, if you contact support, find the report
for your account. These reports are used only to find and fix faults. They do
not include your name, your email address, your quiz answers, your progress,
your payment details, or the contents of any screen.
```

**Why.** Today crash reporting is mentioned once, in passing, under §4
Third-Party Sharing — there is no collection category for it. Declaring
*Diagnostics / Crash Data* in App Store Connect and *Crash logs* in Play with
nothing in the policy to back it is exactly the mismatch that gets rejected.

The sentence about the account identifier is there because `setUserIdentifier`
was approved on 2026-09-17. It is also why the store answer for crash data must
be **Linked to the user**.

## Edit 3 — extend §2.3 "Information We Do NOT Collect"

**Find:**

> We do not access your GPS/Location, Camera, Microphone, Contacts, or Health data.

**Replace with:**

```
We do not access your GPS or location, camera, photos, microphone, contacts,
phone numbers, SMS or call logs, or health and biometric data.

We do not collect payment card numbers, bank details or billing addresses —
Apple and Google process all payments and act as the merchant of record.

We do not collect advertising identifiers (IDFA or AAID), and we do not track
you across other apps or websites.
```

**Why.** The last line is the positive counterpart to Edit 1 — it is what lets
you answer "no" to every tracking question on both forms and point at the
policy. The payment line matches what §2.1 already implies and makes the
*Financial Info → Payment Info: not collected* answer explicit.

---

## Edit 4 — bump the date

**Find:** `Last Updated: March 17, 2026`
**Replace with:** `Last Updated: September 17, 2026`

The policy's own §9 (or equivalent) promises to update this date when the
policy changes. Reviewers do read it, and a stale date next to new disclosures
looks worse than either alone.

## The underlying problem, for later

There are **two** privacy policies and they have drifted:

| | Where | State |
|---|---|---|
| Google Sites page | `sites.google.com/view/driveusa/privacy-policy` | What users and reviewers read. Was last updated March 2026 |
| `privacy_policy.md` | this repo | More accurate today, longer, and **nobody can see it** |

The repo file was audited and corrected on 2026-09-17 — that work changed
nothing public. The live page also has two things the repo file lacks and which
are real: **IP address** and **Firebase Installation IDs**.

Worth deciding which is canonical. The cheapest durable answer is to host the
repo file (it is Markdown, and `firebase.json` already serves `web/`) and point
the app and both stores at that URL, so there is one document that changes with
the code. Until then, the Google Sites page is the one that counts and the repo
file is a draft.
